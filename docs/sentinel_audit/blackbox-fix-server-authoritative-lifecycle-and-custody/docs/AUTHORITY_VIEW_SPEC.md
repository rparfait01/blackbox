# BLACK BOX — Authority View Specification

**Version:** 1.0
**Status:** Implementation spec + outreach/pitch document
**Audience:** Police dispatch, EMS, fire response, embassy/consular staff, security agencies

---

## 1. Executive summary

When a BLACK BOX activation is shared with an authority, the dashboard re-renders into a **dispatch-grade situation report** — one-line summary, multi-format coordinates, live audio, callback path to the witness, and a one-tap "I am responding" acknowledgment that notifies every other viewer in the chain.

The goal is not to replace dispatch. The goal is to give the human dispatcher **30 seconds of head start**. They already have a job to do; this is a tool that gives them better raw material to do it with.

---

## 2. The problem this view exists to solve

Today, when a friend or family member calls emergency services about a missing or endangered person, the dispatcher has nothing but the caller's secondhand panic. Reports describe:

- *"My friend isn't answering her phone."*
- *"I think something happened to my brother — he was meeting someone he didn't know."*
- *"Her location stopped updating an hour ago."*

The dispatcher must then extract location, identity, last-known status, threat type, and contact info from a distraught caller. This takes minutes. In an active emergency, minutes are everything.

**With BLACK BOX shared into authority view, the dispatcher sees:**

- A one-line dispatch summary already structured for CAD entry
- Live coordinates in three notations (decimal, DMS, Plus Code)
- Live audio of what's actually happening
- The forwarding witness's name and reverse-callback number
- AI-generated threat classification with confidence
- Subject identity (if user pre-registered)

The dispatcher reads, copies to CAD, and dispatches. The transformation is from "interview a distraught civilian" to "read a structured incident report."

## 3. Access tiers — how an authority gets in

Three paths, in order of friction:

### Tier A: Link forwarded by a designated contact
A trusted contact who received the original alert taps **"Share with police"** in the share sheet. This action:
1. Dials the local emergency number (110 in Japan, 911 US, 112 EU, etc.) via the user's phone
2. Copies the BLACK BOX link to the clipboard
3. The caller, while on the line with dispatch, says *"I'm sending you a link — paste it from clipboard, it shows everything live"* — or texts the link to a dispatcher-provided number

Dispatcher opens the link → authority view loads.

**Authentication:** Implicit. The contact is the gatekeeper. The link itself is an authority-tagged token. No login required from dispatch's side; they already proved authority by being the operator on a call back to the original phone.

### Tier B: Verified authority partner integration
For jurisdictions that opt in (pilot agencies, future integrations):
- Pre-shared API key or OAuth flow integrates with the dispatcher's CAD / PSAP system
- Authority-tier links auto-open in their internal interface
- Audit trail flows back to BLACK BOX showing officer ID, agency, response time
- More secure, more auditable, slower to roll out

### Tier C: Direct via search (future, gated)
Sworn officers with verified credentials can search active BLACK BOX events in their jurisdiction. This is a v2+ feature that requires regulatory framework, agency MOUs, and significant abuse controls. Out of scope for v1.

**For MVP, ship Tier A only.** It works through any existing dispatch system without integration cost.

## 4. What the authority view shows

The dashboard renders differently when the URL token is authority-tagged. Specifically:

### 4.1 Dispatch summary block (the headline)

A single paragraph at the top of the screen, generated by the user's AI provider as soon as enough signal is available:

> *"Yumi Tanaka, 28, activated 04:32 ago. Currently moving northeast at 1.2 m/s, approximately 3.2 km from registered home. Two voices detected — Japanese, male aggressor present, weapon language ('knife') mentioned at 02:14 elapsed. Subject is registered female, 28, 160cm, wearing dark jacket per most recent self-description. Nearest police station: Naha Central, 1.8 km, estimated response 4 minutes. Local emergency: 110."*

**Single tap to copy to clipboard for CAD entry.** Structured to fit any CAD free-text incident field.

### 4.2 Multi-format coordinates

Decimal, Degrees-Minutes-Seconds, and Plus Code displayed simultaneously. Each individually tap-to-copy.

```
26.3344°N · 127.7894°E        (decimal, default for most dispatch)
26°20'03.8"N 127°47'21.8"E    (DMS, Google Maps compatible)
PMG3+RQ Naha                  (Plus Code, Google Plus Codes — increasingly used)
```

### 4.3 Live audio stream

Real-time streaming audio with timestamp scrubbing. Authority can rewind to hear what happened 30 seconds, 2 minutes, 5 minutes ago. Audio is forensic-grade if from v2 hardware, phone-grade otherwise. Always with timestamps overlaid.

### 4.4 Live transcript with classification

Rolling transcript with speaker diarization (Voice 1 / Voice 2 / Voice 3...). Aggressor speech tagged distinctly. Detected threat vocabulary highlighted.

### 4.5 Subject identification

If the user pre-registered descriptors:
- Photo (most recent profile)
- Physical descriptors (height, build, hair)
- Today's self-description (clothing, vehicle, last known direction) if entered
- Medical conditions relevant to response (e.g., allergic to certain medications)
- Languages spoken

If no pre-registration: blank, just initials and activation timestamp.

### 4.6 Forwarding witness contact

Critical for follow-up:
- Witness name (the contact who forwarded the link)
- Phone number (one-tap dial)
- LINE/WhatsApp/messaging handle (one-tap message)
- Relationship to subject (friend, family, partner, colleague)

The dispatcher can call the witness back for additional context without leaving the dashboard.

### 4.7 Nearest response resources

Auto-calculated from coordinates:
- Nearest police station (with phone number, distance, estimated response time)
- Nearest hospital with emergency capability
- Nearest embassy / consulate (if subject is foreign national, derived from registration)
- Nearest fire station (for any incident type that may require it)

### 4.8 Threat classification

AI-generated, with confidence:
- Two voices · One aggressor (confidence: 89%)
- Compliance language detected (confidence: 76%)
- Weapon mention: knife (confidence: 92%)
- Sexual assault language: not detected
- Vehicle audio detected (confidence: 68%)
- Direction of aggressor voice: 45° NE (only on v2 hardware)

Threat level: CRITICAL / HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW / UNKNOWN

### 4.9 Device status (when v1 or v2 hardware present)

- Hardware tier (Sentinel cube / Recorder puck)
- Device battery
- Connection quality
- Storage status (v2: on-device SSD usage)
- Tamper events with timestamps
- Last-gasp packet receipt (if applicable)

### 4.10 Audit trail

For evidentiary purposes:
- Activation timestamp (cryptographically signed)
- Continuous chain of audio/location chunks (each timestamped, hash-chained)
- Every viewer (token, time opened, time of last activity, IP if accessible)
- Every share (who shared, to which channel, when)
- Every authority action ("I am responding" acks, status updates)

This is the data that holds up in court.

## 5. Actions available in authority view

The authority view exposes specific verbs the dispatcher / officer needs:

### "I am responding"
Primary action. Single tap announces to every viewer in the chain that authority is en route. Affects the public/social view ("4 registered responders en route"), affects the user's dashboard (if they're checking via hidden gesture: "Help is coming").

### "Copy dispatch summary"
Copies the structured paragraph to clipboard, ready for CAD entry. Most dispatchers will use this.

### "Call witness"
Initiates call back to the forwarding contact for additional context. The dispatcher's phone dials directly.

### "Mark as ongoing"
Adds a status flag. Affects the dashboard rendering — others see "Authority engaged."

### "Mark as resolved"
Ends the event from authority side. Triggers stand-down workflow, archive, audit log close-out.

### "Request elevated data"
For sensitive details requiring authentication — full identity history, prior activations, family contact tree. Tier B-only action; requires verified credentials.

### "Add notes"
Authority can append timestamped notes that become part of the audit log. *"Unit 47 dispatched 18:42. Subject located 18:51 at junction X. Aggressor in custody 18:58."*

### "Download evidence package"
At any point, generate a signed evidence package — audio, video (if any), transcript, location trail, classification log, share history. ZIP file. Court-admissible chain of custody.

## 6. Privacy and legal framework

The authority view exposes a substantial amount of data. Several guardrails are non-negotiable:

### 6.1 No retention beyond event lifecycle
Authority view access is scoped to the active event. Tokens expire 6 hours after event resolution by default (user-configurable up to 30 days for active investigations).

### 6.2 Every access is logged
Each authority viewer's session is logged: when opened, what was viewed, what actions taken, IP address. Available to the user as part of the audit trail.

### 6.3 No bulk export without legal process
The "Download evidence package" action requires the user's authorization or a court order. Default behavior is to require user signature (digital, via SMS link).

### 6.4 No subject identity without pre-registration
If the user chose not to register a photo or physical descriptors, dispatch sees nothing personal. Just coordinates, audio, classification. The user controls what dispatch can see.

### 6.5 Clear disclosure to user
On activation, the user (during dormant state) sees a one-line note: *"In an emergency, your designated contacts can share access with authorities. You can revoke at any time."*

### 6.6 Right to delete after event
After event resolution, the user can elect to delete all data (audio, video, transcript) from cloud storage. Audit metadata (timestamps, who viewed, when) is preserved for legal/evidentiary purposes — actual recordings can be wiped on user request.

## 7. Integration paths

Three integration depths for jurisdictions that want to formalize:

### 7.1 No integration (Tier A default)
Authority opens the link in a browser. Dashboard renders. No agency-side code changes required. **Ships day one.**

### 7.2 Webhook integration
Agency provides a webhook URL. BLACK BOX backend posts structured event data (JSON) to the webhook when a link is shared into authority view. Agency's existing CAD or dispatch system parses and creates an incident record automatically.

**Setup:** ~1 day for the agency's IT. **Cost:** Free.

### 7.3 Full PSAP / NG911 integration
For agencies running modern Next Generation 911 (NG911) or equivalent international systems. BLACK BOX events can be ingested as i3 PIDF-LO compliant location objects, with audio streams via SIP/RTP. Officer interface becomes BLACK BOX-native — no separate browser tab needed.

**Setup:** Requires technical engagement, typically 6–12 weeks for pilot. Compatible with major CAD vendors (Tyler New World, Hexagon, Motorola PremierOne, CentralSquare).

**Cost:** Free for the agency. Revenue is the underlying product sales.

## 8. Pitching to authorities

The conversation with a police chief, dispatch supervisor, or emergency operations manager goes something like:

> *"BLACK BOX is a personal safety device people are already buying. When they're in trouble, they activate it, and it streams live audio, video, and location to their family. Their family can share that link with you. You'd see a structured dispatch summary, exact coordinates, live audio of what's happening, and a callback number for the witness. You don't have to install anything. You don't have to integrate anything. When the link comes in, you open it. We can either send you a sample, or schedule a 10-minute demo on a sandbox event."*

The pitch isn't "use our system." The pitch is "your dispatchers will sometimes receive these links anyway, because civilians are already using the product. Here's how to make use of what's in them."

### Suggested early outreach targets

In order of likely receptivity:

1. **Tourism-heavy jurisdictions.** Okinawa Prefectural Police, Tokyo Metropolitan Police tourist beats, Kyoto, Hokkaido prefectures. Foreign tourist safety is an active policy concern post-COVID reopening.
2. **Campus security at universities.** Especially universities with significant international student populations. Lower barrier than full police, often eager for tech adoption.
3. **Embassy / consular emergency lines.** Particularly US, UK, Australian, Canadian consulates in Japan / Southeast Asia. Their citizens are often the ones using BLACK BOX abroad.
4. **NGO partners.** Domestic violence response organizations, women's safety NGOs, journalist safety organizations (CPJ, RSF). These are advocacy paths that pressure formal authority adoption.
5. **Private security firms.** Executive protection, corporate security, event security. Smaller decision loops, easier pilots, possible B2B revenue side-channel.

### Pilot program structure

A 90-day pilot with one agency:
- Free access to authority view for all activations forwarded by their jurisdiction's contacts
- BLACK BOX provides training materials (15-minute video, 2-page reference card)
- BLACK BOX provides a dedicated support contact during the pilot
- At day 90: review what worked, what didn't, what response time improvements were measurable

Agency commits no money. BLACK BOX commits training time and engineering responsiveness.

## 9. Implementation phases (engineering)

Aligned with the main software spec.

| Phase | Scope |
|---|---|
| **Phase A** (concurrent with W7 dashboard) | Authority-tagged tokens in token tier system. Dispatch summary AI prompt template. Multi-format coordinates display. |
| **Phase B** (1-2 weeks after W7) | Reverse-callback to witness. Nearest response resources lookup (police/hospital/embassy via OpenStreetMap data). Subject identification render conditional on user pre-registration. |
| **Phase C** (post-MVP) | Authority actions ("I am responding," "Add notes," "Download evidence package"). Audit trail export. Cryptographic event signing. |
| **Phase D** (post-MVP, partner-driven) | Webhook integration for agency CAD systems. Documented JSON event schema. Sandbox environment for agency testing. |
| **Phase E** (year 2+) | Full NG911 / PSAP integration. Compliance certifications (FedRAMP for US gov-adjacent agencies, equivalent in other jurisdictions). |

## 10. Open questions for legal review

Before going to actual authorities, these need answers from a lawyer with relevant jurisdiction expertise:

1. **What's the liability exposure** if a dispatcher relies on BLACK BOX classification and the classification is wrong? (Recommended posture: classification is advisory, dispatch always makes the final call, system disclaimer accordingly.)
2. **Can a private company legally provide an "authority view" without formal agency designation?** Likely yes in most jurisdictions because the user is voluntarily sharing their own data, but worth confirming per-region.
3. **Wiretap law implications.** Recording one's own conversation is legal in most jurisdictions (one-party consent). Streaming that recording to authorities is also generally legal but specifics vary. Need clear consent disclosure to the user.
4. **GDPR / APPI compliance for the data flowing through Cloudflare and the AI providers.** User is the data controller; BYOK helps; need a clear DPA template for enterprise/agency partners.
5. **Subject identification when subject is a minor.** Different rules in most jurisdictions. Probably gate behind parental account registration for users under 18.

## 11. The honest framing for pitches

Don't oversell. Don't claim the system replaces dispatch. Don't promise outcomes. The honest pitch is:

> "A little information is better than no information going into an unknown situation. We give your dispatchers a head start — coordinates, live audio, who's already there, and what AI thinks is happening. Your dispatchers and officers still do their jobs. They just do them with better raw material."

That framing also dramatically reduces the liability surface. BLACK BOX is an information service feeding human decision-makers, not an autonomous emergency dispatch service.
